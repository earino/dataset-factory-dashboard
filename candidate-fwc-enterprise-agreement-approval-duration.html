The question

what a model is asked to predict

For an enterprise agreement approval application lodged with the Fair Work Commission (FWC) - published in the FWC 'Agreements in progress' list about 2 business days after lodgement, with agreement title, industry and application type visible - will the Commission approve it within a fixed window of lodgement (e.g. the 21-day standard timeframe; the publisher's own statistics report a 15-day median for 2021-22 approvals) rather than later? Prospective users: employers, unions and bargaining consultants timing pay-increase commencement (agreements start 7 days after approval unless a later date is set) and IR researchers studying administrative approval friction. A useful prediction prices the delay at lodgement instead of waiting the ~2-6 weeks for the decision.

What you predict

target definition

Binary on approval latency: 1 if approval date is within N days of lodgement (N fixed from the measured latency distribution), 0 if later; withdrawn/refused applications would be censored, not negative. Label source: approval date and lodgement date in the FWC bulk agreement Excel lists. POPULATION CAVEAT measured at discovery: the bulk lists are the approved-agreements register ('Search by agreement title to find all agreements approved by the Fair Work Commission'); the in-progress list is live-only; refused/withdrawn applications surface only through decision records. A duration task built from the bulk lists alone is therefore conditioned on eventual approval (survivorship) unless non-approved applications are recovered from decisions - a design problem for any reopening, on top of the licence blocker.

When the prediction is made

the information cut-off

Instant = the application's publication in the FWC 'Agreements in progress' list (per the publisher: 'When you apply to us to approve an enterprise agreement or a section 210 variation... we add details to Agreements in progress. This is usually 2 business days after we receive the application.'). Features visible then: agreement title, industry, application type (new approval vs s.210 variation), status. The approval decision is published later - the agreement register records an approval date, and FWC statistics report approval-time distributions, so the outcome cannot exist at the instant. NOT ASSESSED beyond structure: the licence veto (class D) came first, and no historical archive of the in-progress list was verified.
