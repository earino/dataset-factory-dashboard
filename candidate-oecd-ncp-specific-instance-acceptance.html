The question

what a model is asked to predict

At the filing instant of a specific instance - the day an interested party (individual, NGO, trade union, business or other submitter) submits a complaint to a National Contact Point (NCP) alleging that a multinational enterprise did not observe the OECD Guidelines - will the NCP accept the case for further examination at initial assessment (Step 3 of the procedure), or will the case be closed without proceeding ('not accepted', which per the publisher's own terminology also includes withdrawals before the initial assessment completes)? A useful prediction tells complainants whether and how a grievance framed this way is likely to clear the intake gate of the chosen NCP, tells companies and RBC observers which complaint profiles are likely to enter mediation, and gives the 52-NCP network a measurable intake-consistency signal across the functional-equivalence agenda the OECD itself tracks. Acceptance is a real institutional decision published 1-12+ months after filing (measured: 2021-2025 closed-cohort acceptance 54-63%), not an editorial artefact.

What you predict

target definition

Binary label per specific instance: 1 = the NCP accepted the case for further examination at initial assessment (DB Status values indicating acceptance: 'Concluded' after acceptance, or an in-progress-but-accepted state; the publisher's status taxonomy from the case records and the annual reports: closed = concluded OR not accepted, in progress = awaiting initial assessment OR accepted and ongoing); 0 = the case did not proceed past initial assessment ('Not accepted', which the publisher defines to include cases withdrawn prior to completion of the initial assessment). Source columns: the DB case-record 'Status' field (label source; excludable - not shipped as a feature) with 'Outcomes', 'Procedural issues' and all prose summaries excluded from features as post-hoc. Rows with Status 'In progress' at extraction are excluded from scored splits (censored, not negative). Label derivation needs no external data and no threshold.

When the prediction is made

the information cut-off

One sentence: the prediction is made on the submission date of the specific instance (day grain; the NCP-recorded 'Date' field on the case record), using only the complaint's own DB-coded attributes (lead NCP, supporting NCP, country of the issue, submitter class, sector/subsector, Guidelines topics and subtopics cited, entity-involved type) plus trailing history computed only from cases whose initial assessment was already decided before that date, and the label is the initial-assessment decision that the NCP publishes strictly afterwards. Dated example (Europe/Paris, publisher HQ; all dates recorded by the publisher at day grain, no time of day): case kr0029, Hoang Gia Trading & Services vs Lotte Corporation; feature event window = all specific instances with submission date < 2026-02-27 and decision-published before the instant (trailing acceptance rates, counts per NCP/submitter/topic); first-available timestamps and version = the complaint itself exists 2026-02-27 (Korean NCP, filed 2026-02-27 local; the DB's own listing date for this case equals the filing date - measured, see measurements.db_listing_lag), and the case's structured attributes are as coded in the current database vintage (fetched 2026-11-03; the database migrated platform in June 2025 and no historical vintages are archived - current-vintage posture, explicit assumption); eligibility cutoff = submission date in a scored window AND terminal status at the extraction vintage ('In progress' cases are excluded, not labelled); prediction instant = 2026-02-27 00:00 Europe/Paris (day-grain convention stated); target start/end = the initial assessment event, published 2026-05-21 (Korean NCP statement, decision 'not accepted'), i.e. target window 2026-02-27..2026-05-21 ending at the decision - every part of the target window is after the instant; the DB listing date (2026-02-27 for this case) is never used as instant or decision date. For any future-event scoring, a row scored at filing must use only rows decided strictly before its filing date for history features.
