The question

what a model is asked to predict

At the tender-publication instant of a Kenyan public procurement process published on the Public Procurement Information Portal (PPIP), using only at-publication attributes (buyer entity, procurement method, main procurement category, award criteria, title text, tender-period dates), predict whether the process will end with at least one published contract award within a censoring window after the tender period closes, versus no award ever published (a mixture of cancellation, retender and non-publication that the portal does not distinguish - tender.status is null on 6,727/6,727 sampled 2018 records). A useful prediction would support PPRA's own compliance-monitoring question - which published tenders silently die - and citizen-oversight prioritisation. The licence veto recorded below was established before any task probing; no novelty claim is made in either direction.

What you predict

target definition

Binary: >=1 published award on the process within the censoring window vs none. Label source column: awards array of the OCDS record (excludable by construction). Right-censoring (processes whose window has not closed) excluded by eligibility cutoff. Measured on the OCP mirror's 2018 year-file: 2,074/6,727 records (30.8%) carry >=1 award; 4,653 carry none. tender.status is null throughout, so no explicit cancelled status exists in this publication.

When the prediction is made

the information cut-off

The tender-publication instant (release 'date' / tender first appearing in PPIP); only forward evidence of the outcome window follows it. Not further specified because the lead was closed at the licence gate.
