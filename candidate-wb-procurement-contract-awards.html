The question

what a model is asked to predict

The task family this source could carry: at the end of a quarter, given a WB IPF project's contract-award history and attributes, predict whether the project will sign another major contract award in the next one to two quarters (award occurrence / pipeline continuation), or predict the composition of the next award (local vs foreign supplier country, procurement method, category). Prospective users would be firms/consultants watching the WB procurement pipeline and observers of implementation slowdowns. DROPPED before construction: the stream carries no post-award outcome, so every derivable label is future administrative activity on the same borrower-entered stream - the target family measured twice on WB instruments with no headroom over identity/rate tables (see rejection_reason).

What you predict

target definition

No defensible target exists in this stream: the documented 21-column schema (DS00005, read 2026-11-03) contains only at-award attributes (project, borrower country, procurement category/method, supplier, supplier country, contract amount, signing date, WB contract number) and the snapshot date; there is no post-signature field (no revision, modification, termination, completion or end-date column), and the publisher states contracts 'may be modified after signature' with modifications not reflected. Any label must therefore be a future award occurrence or composition on the same stream - administrative activity, not an externally observable outcome.

When the prediction is made

the information cut-off

One sentence (moot, recorded for the rejection): the prediction would be made at the end of fiscal quarter T using awards with contract_signing_date <= that instant, about awards signed in T+1..T+2; contract_signing_date is a documented at-award column and the as_of_date snapshot column is the publisher's refresh stamp, not an outcome; the FY2001-2016 segment is prior-reviewed-only while FY2020+ is all IPF awards, so the population definition breaks across segments and pre-FY2020 history cannot serve as the same population for FY2020+ rows.
