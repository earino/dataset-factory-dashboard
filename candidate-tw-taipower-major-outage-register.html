The question

what a model is asked to predict

For a major distribution outage in the Taipower flash-report register (occurrence time t, Asia/Taipei), will restoration (復電時間) take more than 60 minutes from t? Retrospective only: the register is published yearly, months after the events, so no operational real-time claim is possible.

What you predict

target definition

Binary: restoration duration = 復電時間 - 發生時間 > 60 minutes (2024 vintage class balance at that threshold: 50/102 = 49.0%; 2023 archived vintage: 51/91 = 56.0%). Label source columns 復電時間 (and 發生時間), excludable from features by construction. Register-level defect measured in the 2023 archived vintage: 6/91 rows have 復電時間 earlier than 發生時間 (systematic ~12-hour clock misentries on the same date, listed in notes.md); the 2024 vintage has 0. Population censoring: the register is not all outages - it carries only cases meeting the four published major-incident criteria (quoted in notes.md), so an occurrence formulation has no enumerable negative population from this source; duration on reported cases is the only derivable label.

When the prediction is made

the information cut-off

NOT SUPPORTED as an operational instant - the decisive timing finding. The publisher's own dataset-page replies state the register is updated once a year with the previous year's data, deliberately delayed to limit reuse (quote in license_evidence and notes.md). Within a clearly-labelled retrospective formulation the instant is the row's 發生時間 (occurrence) itself: features are the register fields fixed at occurrence (occurrence datetime, 事故名稱 incident name, 停電戶數 households as final-register vintage values - a documented-vintage caveat), and the target window is (occurrence, restoration]. Measured publication evidence: resource last updated 2025-06-16 carrying 2024 events only; a Wayback capture from 2026-09-29 still carried the 2024 vintage, so the 2025 update had not landed 15 months after year end - the yearly channel is not dependable either.
