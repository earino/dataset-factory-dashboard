packaged · CA · 2026-11-07 09:50 UTC

The question

what a model is asked to predict

At Alberta Wildfire's recorded initial assessment of a newly discovered wildfire (assessment_datetime, 'originally assessed'), predict whether the wildfire will escape initial attack - final area burned at least 100 ha (variants at 4/40/200 ha) - and/or remain uncontained more than 72 hours (time from assessment to first under control), using only the crew-observed assessment record (size at assessment, rate of spread, fire behaviour type, position on slope, in-situ weather over the fire, FBP fuel type), pre-instant timing fields (estimated ignition, reported, detection channel), and calendar/geography. A useful prediction supports the actual initial-attack resourcing decision - airtanker/crew prioritization and same-day escalation - which the agency itself states happens at this instant.

What you predict

target definition

Primary label, binary: escape = final area burned (current_size, the publisher's 'final area burned of the wildfire') at or above the threshold (primary 100 ha; variants 4/40/200 ha measured). Secondary label, binary: containment = first under control (first_uc_date, 'sufficient suppression action to ensure no further spread') later than 72 hours after assessment_datetime; 42.0% of fires were already under control at the assessment instant (assessment == first_uc, a degenerate label-0 stratum the construction decision must handle and declare). Both labels derive entirely from the record's own post-instant fields; both source columns are excludable from what ships. Population: all wildfires in Alberta's Forest Protection Area, 2006-2025, one row per wildfire ((YEAR, FIRE_NUMBER) unique, measured); eligibility is assessment-based, not outcome-based (100% of discovered wildfires are assessed).

When the prediction is made

the information cut-off

The initial assessment instant, assessment_datetime, documented by the publisher as 'the date and time the wildfire was originally assessed by Alberta Wildfire employees'. Every feature field's meaning is documented at that instant: assessment_hectares, fire_spread_rate, fire_type, fire_position_on_slope, the five in-situ weather fields and fuel_type all carry the publisher's wording 'at the time of initial assessment'; reported_date/discovered_date/fire_start_date/detection fields are documented pre-instant and measured never to fall after the instant (27,828/27,828 for reported_date). Post-instant families are excluded by design and measurement: cause classification (dictionary: 'under investigation' temporary cause, final cause updated later), dispatch block (2.41% of dispatch timestamps fall after the assessment - mixed ordering, so excluded), all suppression/milestone fields (initial_action_by, ia_*, fire_fighting_start_*, bucketing, first_bh/uc/to/ex) and the label sources (current_size, size_class). Timestamps carry no timezone; they are read as local Alberta operational time (America/Edmonton) - a declared assumption to be confirmed at construction and stated in quality.json.evaluation_contract.
