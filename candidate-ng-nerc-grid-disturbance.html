The question

what a model is asked to predict

At a day-ahead instant, predict whether the Nigerian national grid will record a total or partial system collapse on the following day, from published grid-operating history (generation, frequency, load). A useful prediction would support the system operator's and large consumers' preparedness decisions. Closed at the licence gate before any task probing; the secondary task-shape and timing observations below are from the publisher's published report text, not from a constructed dataset.

What you predict

target definition

Binary (collapse day vs non-collapse day; optionally total vs partial). Label source: NERC's unit-level system-collapse event tables in quarterly and annual reports (date, time, type, immediate cause, restoration date/time; e.g. 2025 Annual Report Table 24 'System Collapses in 2019 - 2025'). Event-grain positives are sparse and declining: NERC order NERC/2023/035 (published figures) lists partial/total 2016: 6/22, 2017: 9/15, 2018: 1/12, 2019: 1/10, 2020: 0/4, 2021: 2/2, 2022: 2/4, 2023: 0/3; the 2024 Annual Report records 9 incidents in 2024; the 2025 Annual Report records 2; the 2026 Q1 report records 2 (one total 23 Jan, one partial 27 Jan). Roughly 90 events 2016-2025 against ~3,650 days, with positives clustered within crisis years. The deeper timing defect: daily-resolution features are NOT published in bulk - NERC's operational data products are monthly/quarterly aggregates (average daily system frequency by month, average hourly generation) plus the event tables themselves, so a day-ahead operational task cannot be assembled from published features without a feature source that has not been identified.

When the prediction is made

the information cut-off

A day-ahead instant. Not further specified: the licence veto stands on its own, and the feature-timing contract is separately unresolved (see target_definition).
