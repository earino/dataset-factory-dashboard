cbsa-land-border-wait-exceedance

investigating · CA 08:50 UTC

The question

what a model is asked to predict

For a measured CBSA land port of entry P, at instant T = the publication timestamp of one of CBSA's hourly border-wait-time snapshots, using only hourly snapshots of the archive with timestamps <= T (port identity, hour-of-day, day-of-week, season, recent wait history and status vocabulary), will the port's traveller flow exceed the CBSA highway-traveller service standard at ANY hourly snapshot in the next 24 hours, i.e. the window (T, T+24h]? A useful prediction lets a traveller or dispatcher re-time or re-route a crossing a day ahead, the same decision CBSA's own CanBorder app supports with forecasts; it also lets CBSA compare ports against its published 95% compliance target.

What you predict

target definition

Binary: 1 if the port's Travellers Flow cell at ANY hourly snapshot in (T, T+24h] exceeds the CBSA highway-traveller service standard; 0 if no valid traveller cell in the window exceeds it. Label source: the Travellers Flow cells of the target window's snapshots; they are excluded from solver-visible features by a common-cutoff design (no shipped row may expose another scored row's outcome - the abs-visitor/canadabuys blind-join defect class is a named gate check). Threshold rule: the publisher's own service standard, whose documented forms are 10 minutes on weekdays (Mon-Thu) and 20 minutes on weekends (Fri-Sun) and holidays for FY2014-15..FY2017-18, and from FY2018-19 additionally 'July to August: 20 minutes (7 days a week)' (all quoted from CBSA service-standard pages, current and Wayback-pinned, see measurements.service_standard_evolution). Construction picks ONE of: (a) era-correct full rule (needs the FY2010-11..FY2013-14 forms verified - named prerequisite; FY2014-15 back is verified), or (b) the era-stable core rule 10/20 weekday/weekend-holiday everywhere (conservative, verified FY2014-15..present), or (c) a fixed plain threshold (e.g. >20 min) if the standard-linked rule proves unstable. Valid label denominator: traveller cells whose value is a minute count or 'No delay' (dictionary: 'No delay' = five minutes or less). 'Closed' (outside operating hours), 'Not applicable' (business line unavailable) and 'Missed entry' (no wait recorded) are not outcomes: an (office, hour) whose whole target window has no valid traveller cell is ineligible and right-censored out, never imputed; eligibility is decided from information at T (the port's own status history) not from the outcome. In-hour duplicate rows (publisher-acknowledged) are collapsed by a deterministic rule fixed before scoring: label = exceedance of ANY reported value in the (office, hour) group ('any' semantics; a conservative choice for a warning-use task, robust to the measured duplicate conflict pattern).

When the prediction is made

the information cut-off

T = the snapshot hour of port P as printed in the archive's 'Updated' column (local-time string with a Canadian tz abbreviation; ADT/AST/EDT/EST/CDT/CST/MDT/MST/PDT/PST all measured in samples, each resolving to a fixed UTC offset; construction normalizes to UTC and states the offset table). Features are archive rows with Updated <= T. The target window (T, T+24h] is strictly after the instant; no part of it precedes T. RETROSPECTIVE, ARCHIVE-VINTAGE framing (recorded, not resolved to operational): the hourly archive is a frozen historical record published in batches (the 2010-2014 file and the 2014-2016Q1 quarterly files all carry CKAN created=2016-12-01; later quarters in batches 2017-03..2020-04; the series ends 2020-03-31 - Wayback capture 2021-11-23 shows 24 quarterly resources ending 2020-01..2020-03 and no successor, and the monthly service-standard file only appears by 2024-12), so the task claims no operational download availability at T; the files fetched today are one current vintage (the changelog documents FR translation fixes 2017-09-19 and a column rename 2016-07-20 as the only revisions; no EN data-value revision is documented, and current-vintage is the recorded assumption, same posture as the abs-visitor precedent). The self-collection option (hourly fetch of the live feed, ~3.3 KB per fetch, to build a fresh operational tail from now) is recorded as a possible extension, not part of this task.
